Particle Swarm Optimization: A swarm-based search where each particle adjusts its velocity based on its own best position and the swarm’s global best. Inertia (w), cognitive (c_1), and social (c_2) coefficients tune the balance between exploration and convergence. Collective sharing of best positions drives fast discovery of optima.
Ant Colony Optimization: A constructive search that simulates pheromone trails laid by ants exploring paths. Probabilistic decisions favor edges with higher pheromone concentration and shorter path cost; evaporation prevents stagnation. Parameters for pheromone update rate, evaporation coefficient, and heuristic influence determine exploration–exploitation tradeoffs.
Simulated Annealing: A probabilistic global optimizer inspired by metal annealing that accepts uphill (worse) moves with a probability controlled by temperature. The temperature parameter decreases gradually (“cooling schedule”), reducing randomness over time and shifting from exploration to exploitation. This mechanism helps escape local minima early and refine near the global optimum later.
Firefly Algorithm: Models attraction between fireflies proportional to their brightness (fitness) and inversely proportional to distance. The light absorption coefficient (\gamma) controls convergence speed, and the randomization parameter (\alpha) introduces exploration. Over iterations, brighter (better) fireflies attract others, forming clusters around optima.
Genetic Algorithm: An evolutionary optimizer that evolves a population of solutions through selection, crossover, and mutation. High-fitness individuals reproduce more, combining traits into new offspring, while random mutations preserve diversity. Population size, crossover rate, and mutation probability control convergence rate and diversity maintenance.
Differential Evolution: A population-based algorithm that perturbs candidate vectors using scaled differences between other population members. The mutation factor F and crossover rate CR govern exploration and recombination strength. Selection is greedy: trial solutions replace parents only if fitter, ensuring steady progress toward global minima.
Whale Optimization Algorithm: Inspired by humpback whales’ bubble-net hunting, this method alternates between spiral updating (exploitation) and random search (exploration). A decreasing control parameter modulates movement amplitude. The spiral equation models adaptive contraction around the global best, balancing global and local refinement.
Cuckoo Optimization Algorithm: Extends the Cuckoo Search metaphor by modeling cuckoo reproduction, competition, and migration. Cuckoos lay eggs (candidate solutions) in random nests, with survival determined by egg fitness and crowding. Poor eggs are discarded or replaced, while migration between subpopulations ensures diversity. Control parameters include egg-laying radius, survival rate, and migration probability.
Stochastic Diffusion Search: A cooperative agent system where each agent tests a random component of its hypothesis (partial solution). Agents that succeed keep their hypotheses; unsuccessful ones adopt hypotheses from successful peers. This partial evaluation and diffusion mechanism rapidly spread promising solutions across the population, requiring few full evaluations.
Bacterial Colony Optimization: Simulates chemotaxis, reproduction, and elimination–dispersal behaviors of bacteria. Agents tumble and swim toward nutrient gradients (improving fitness), replicate if successful, and occasionally disperse randomly to avoid stagnation. Parameters like chemotactic step size and elimination probability govern convergence stability and exploration extent.
Fruit Fly Optimization Algorithm: Models olfactory-guided search and visual localization of fruit flies. Flies perform random “smell-based” exploration to identify regions of high odor concentration (estimated fitness), then switch to “vision-based” refinement near the best location. Key parameters include population size and step length scaling between smell and sight phases.
Krill Herd Algorithm: Updates positions via social interaction, foraging, and random diffusion. Social attraction guides krill toward better neighbors, foraging directs motion toward known food sources (personal and global bests), and diffusion adds stochastic motion. The movement coefficients balance exploration and convergence precision.
Migrating Birds Optimization: Emulates the V-formation flight of migrating birds with periodic leader rotation. The leader (best solution) performs intensified local search, while followers adjust positions relative to it. After fixed iterations, leadership changes to a new bird, maintaining diversity and preventing premature convergence.
Backtracking Search Algorithm: Uses a main population and an archive of past solutions to generate trial vectors via scaled differential mutation. The “backtracking” refers to exploiting information from previous generations. With few control parameters (scale F, crossover rate CR), BSA achieves robust convergence on continuous domains.
Black Hole Algorithm: Represents the best solution as a black hole attracting all others (stars). Each star moves linearly toward the black hole; stars crossing the event horizon are replaced by new random solutions. The algorithm iteratively improves the black hole as better stars emerge, balancing attraction and diversification.
Dolphin Echolocation: Uses sonar-like probing to explore the search space. Each dolphin adjusts wavelength and pulse intensity to alternate between wide-area scanning (exploration) and fine-resolution focusing (exploitation). Collective communication ensures that discovered high-fitness echoes guide other dolphins toward optimal zones.
Animal Migration Optimization: Models population groups migrating between habitats. Individuals periodically migrate based on fitness and group performance, introducing genetic diversity through exchange. Migration size and frequency govern exploration strength, while local adaptation within groups refines exploitation.
Keshtel Algorithm: Based on greedy bird foraging, where individuals rapidly exploit discovered food sources while a subset explores new regions. Agents move toward locally rich areas (high fitness) but occasionally abandon them to maintain global coverage. A greediness factor controls exploitation intensity versus exploratory diversification.
SDA Optimization Algorithm: Smell Detection Agents follow virtual odor gradients to locate fitness peaks. Agents stochastically sample surrounding air and move toward higher scent concentrations, sharing implicit odor trails. Step size and scent sensitivity parameters regulate gradient tracking accuracy and avoidance of local traps.
Artificial Root Foraging Algorithm: Simulates adaptive root growth toward nutrient-rich zones. Roots branch and elongate in directions with higher fitness, while unproductive branches wither (pruning). Branching probability and growth rate govern exploration breadth and intensification depth.
Bumble Bees Mating Optimization: Models a queen bee mating flight where mating probability depends on fitness difference and flight energy. Offspring are generated by recombining queen and drone traits with mutation introducing novelty. Energy decay and mating rate tune exploration intensity and convergence speed.
Chicken Swarm Optimization: Divides population into hierarchical roles—roosters (best), hens, and chicks—each updating positions via different rules. Roosters explore randomly, hens follow leaders with perturbations, and chicks follow hens closely. Role reassignment each iteration maintains dynamic social balance and prevents premature convergence.
Colliding Bodies Optimization: Treats solutions as elastic bodies colliding and exchanging momentum. After each collision, poorer solutions gain velocity toward better ones while better solutions adjust slightly. No tuning parameters beyond population size are required; repeated collisions drive convergence toward equilibrium (optimum).
Coral Reefs Optimization: Models reef colonization where solutions (corals) reproduce sexually (crossover) or asexually (mutation) and compete for limited settlement space. Stronger corals occupy empty or weaker slots, while overcrowding triggers mortality. Population density and reproduction rates control diversity and convergence pressure.
Radial Movement Optimization: Uses particles moving radially around a dynamic center point, exploring multiple directions simultaneously. The search radius shrinks over time, focusing exploration around updated centers based on best fitness. Angular sampling and radius decay rates control convergence accuracy.
Spider Monkey Optimization: Implements fission–fusion group dynamics, splitting the swarm into subgroups with local leaders and one global leader. Monkeys follow leaders within subgroups for local search, then periodically regroup or reform to renew exploration. Leadership switching prevents stagnation and maintains balanced search.
Soccer League Competition: Represents candidate solutions as teams competing in matches. Winning teams (better fitness) adopt incremental strategy refinements, while losers adapt successful strategies from winners. Periodic training and random “player transfers” inject diversity, ensuring robust global optimization.
Artificial Algae Algorithm: Simulates algae photosynthesis and population growth, where energy corresponds to fitness. High-energy individuals reproduce exponentially until reaching colony capacity; weaker ones die. Photosynthesis rate and energy sharing parameters modulate exploration and saturation behavior.
Adaptive Dimensional Search: Dynamically focuses search effort on dimensions most sensitive to fitness changes. Step size and active dimension subsets adapt over time based on variable importance metrics. This selective dimensional focus accelerates convergence in high-dimensional problems with correlated variables.
Alienated Ant Algorithm: Enhances ACO by introducing alienated ants that ignore pheromones, exploring new paths to prevent convergence stagnation. If these ants find superior routes, they reinforce them with pheromone, redirecting the colony. The alienation rate controls the exploration–exploitation balance.
Artificial Fish Swarm Algorithm: Models fish behaviors—prey, swarm, and follow—to balance local improvement and social interaction. Fish move toward better neighbors within visual distance and avoid overcrowding via a crowd factor. Step size, visual range, and crowding threshold govern swarm adaptability and convergence.
Bottlenose Dolphin Optimization: Uses cooperative echolocation and communication among dolphins to locate targets. Each dolphin adjusts sonar pulse parameters and shares strong echo positions with the group, enabling coordinated refinement. Periodic scouting ensures continuous exploration of new regions.
Cricket Algorithm: Encodes candidate solutions as chirping crickets whose sound intensity correlates with fitness. Crickets move toward louder (better) chirps while maintaining distance to avoid clustering. Chirp frequency and attenuation rate define movement responsiveness and convergence speed.
Elephant Search Algorithm: Divides population into clans guided by matriarchs (best local solutions). Elephants update positions toward matriarchs for exploitation, while young males periodically migrate for exploration. Clan size and migration frequency maintain diversity across subpopulations.
Jaguar Algorithm: Alternates between stealthy local movement (stalking) and large jumps (pouncing) toward promising regions. Step size adapts dynamically based on improvement rate, enabling careful local refinement and sudden global exploration. Independent agents cover diverse regions while sharing best discoveries.
Locust Swarm Algorithm: Models dual-phase locust behavior—solitary (random exploration) and gregarious (collective exploitation). Phase transition depends on swarm density or improvement rate. Alignment, attraction, and repulsion parameters control swarm cohesion and adaptability.
Moth–Flame Optimization: Represents moths spiraling around flames (elite solutions) in logarithmic trajectories. Spiral radius and flame count decrease over iterations, concentrating search near top solutions. Randomized spiral motion ensures broad coverage while maintaining convergence stability.
Stochastic Fractal Search: Uses fractal diffusion (Lévy or Brownian motion) to generate self-similar random walks around current solutions. Offspring replace parents if fitter, and diffusion scale reduces over time. Fractal dimensionality and perturbation variance govern exploration scope and convergence precision.
Water Wave Optimization: Models wave propagation, refraction, and breaking to balance global and local search. Propagation adjusts wavelength based on improvement; refraction changes direction near constraints, and breaking resets stagnating waves. Wavelength damping and breaking thresholds determine convergence rate.
Eagle Strategy: Combines a global exploratory phase using Lévy flights with a local exploitation phase using precise refinement (e.g., local search). The two-phase structure decouples exploration and intensification for efficiency. Step scaling and phase-switching frequency are key parameters.
Fire Hawk Optimizer: Simulates firehawks deliberately starting “fires” (large perturbations) to flush out prey (better optima). After perturbation, hawks exploit the disturbed region. Fire intensity and triggering thresholds control when large explorations occur versus focused exploitation.
Flying Fox Optimization: Models nocturnal fruit bat foraging with long exploratory flights and social information sharing. Foxes adjust trajectories toward known fruitful trees (good solutions) based on communicated success. Memory persistence and exploration radius govern global search breadth.
Waterwheel Plant Algorithm: Represents distributed trap mechanisms probing multiple search directions simultaneously. Traps (candidate sub-solvers) close on promising moves and reopen after exploitation, maintaining cyclic multi-directional exploration. Trap sensitivity and reactivation interval tune adaptability.
Energy Valley Optimizer: Simulates particles sliding toward energy minima with controlled kinetic energy. Solutions can “hop” over barriers using residual energy, avoiding local minima. Energy damping and reheating parameters govern exploration persistence and convergence accuracy.
Special Forces Algorithm: Uses a small elite team of agents executing reconnaissance (exploration) and coordinated strikes (exploitation). Agents communicate findings and reassign roles dynamically. Mission phases (search, attack, extraction) ensure adaptive focus between diversification and intensification.
Squid Game Optimizer: Conducts staged elimination rounds among candidate solutions using varying objective filters or competitions. Weak solutions are removed, and survivors undergo recombination and refinement. Game phases and survival probability shape convergence pressure and maintain robustness.
Snow Ablation Optimizer: Represents solutions as snow patches melting at rates proportional to their fitness. Poor (high-energy) solutions melt quickly, feeding improvement to lower areas (better solutions). Melt rate and replenishment cycles control exploration and convergence to global minima.
Spider Wasp Optimization: Models predator–prey interactions where wasps locate, paralyze (exploit), and drag prey (solutions) back to nest. Each capture refines the population’s collective knowledge, while failed hunts promote exploration. Capture radius and relocation frequency tune balance between exploitation and diversity.
Dujiangyan Irrigation System Algorithm: Balances exploitation and exploration via adaptive flow division between main (refinement) and overflow (exploration) channels. Flow proportion changes dynamically based on improvement rate, ensuring stable progress without stagnation. Periodic flushing removes poor solutions, maintaining healthy diversity.
Great Wall Construction Algorithm: Builds optimal solutions incrementally as connected segments (subproblems). Each wall segment is optimized locally and joined under continuity constraints. Overlapping subproblem optimization and merging frequency control convergence consistency and scalability.
Puma Optimizer: Alternates between cautious stalking (small-step local improvement) and rapid pouncing (large exploratory jumps). Step magnitude adapts to fitness change rate, conserving energy (computation) by avoiding fruitless pursuits. Memory of past successful “kill sites” guides refinement.
Walrus Optimizer: Switches between deep-diving exploration (broad search) and surface resting (communication and exploitation). Walruses share discoveries during rest, realigning the next dive’s direction. Dive duration and rest frequency regulate the exploration–exploitation rhythm.
Political Optimizer: Models parties competing for votes (fitness) through policy adjustments (solution updates). Successful policies spread across parties (information sharing), while poor ones disappear. Election cycles (selection) and random new party formations maintain adaptive diversity.
Heap-Based Optimizer: Structures population in a hierarchical heap where higher nodes outperform children. Promotions (swaps) occur when subordinates surpass superiors, propagating improvement upward. Controlled information flow within hierarchy maintains order while supporting localized innovation.
Color Harmony Algorithm: Treats solution elements as colors and adjusts them according to harmony principles—complementary, analogous, or triadic relations. Blending and swapping components emulate artistic balancing. Harmony evaluation guides convergence toward aesthetically (fitness) optimized combinations.
Stochastic Paint Optimizer: Builds solutions iteratively like applying paint strokes, using random perturbations of varying scales. Broad “strokes” explore widely early on, followed by finer local refinements. Stroke variance and adaptation schedule govern transition from exploration to convergence.
Mayfly Optimization Algorithm: Combines PSO-like male swarm movement with GA-style mating between male and female populations. Males explore globally using velocity updates, females move toward attractive males, and crossover produces offspring. Flight coefficients and mating energy determine convergence dynamics.
Giza Pyramids Construction Algorithm: Constructs solutions layer-by-layer, optimizing foundational subcomponents before upper levels. Each layer refines upon previous ones under structural consistency constraints. Layer width reduction over iterations naturally transitions from coarse to fine optimization.
Flying Squirrel Search Algorithm: Divides search into “summer” exploration (long gliding jumps) and “winter” exploitation (refining stored caches). Seasonal switching and memory of good caches emulate adaptive resource use. Glide length and seasonal duration parameters control search intensity and stability.
Social Engineering Optimizer: Models persuasion-based information transfer where strong (fit) individuals influence weaker ones’ beliefs (positions). Influence magnitude depends on target gullibility and persuader strength. Controlled stochastic resistance prevents total homogenization, sustaining exploration.
Emperor Penguin Optimizer: Mimics penguin huddling, where individuals move toward warmer (better) central spots while the huddle radius contracts. Spiral or rotational motion enables balanced exploration. Temperature gradient and contraction rate parameters drive convergence precision.
Socio-Evolution and Learning Optimization: Integrates evolutionary reproduction with direct social learning among individuals. Learners partially adopt traits from superior peers while maintaining mutation-driven diversity. Learning rate and selection intensity balance convergence speed and population diversity.
Future Search Algorithm: Incorporates predictive foresight by evaluating simulated future states (multi-step lookahead) before executing a move. Step projection depth and prediction accuracy control efficiency and robustness. The algorithm avoids short-sighted greediness, enabling more stable global convergence.
Emperor Penguins Colony Algorithm: Extends EPO by managing multiple huddles (subpopulations) that periodically merge or migrate toward warmer colonies. Seasonal cycles alternate between tight local convergence and wide re-dispersion. Inter-colony migration rates maintain global exploration capacity.
Tabu Search: A memory-based local search that maintains a tabu list of recently visited solutions or moves to prevent cycling. The algorithm explores beyond local optima by temporarily forbidding reverse steps but allows exceptions for outstanding improvements. Key parameters include tabu tenure and aspiration criteria, balancing diversification with intensification.
Harmony Search: Treats solutions as harmonies in a music improvisation process, combining memory-based exploitation and random exploration. The harmony memory consideration rate (HMCR) and pitch adjustment rate (PAR) control whether new harmonies borrow from existing ones or introduce variation. The population iteratively converges to high-quality harmonies.
Memetic Algorithm: A hybrid of genetic algorithms and local search where each offspring undergoes additional refinement before selection. It merges global recombination with problem-specific exploitation, often accelerating convergence. Local search depth and frequency are critical hyperparameters affecting tradeoffs between speed and computational cost.
Clonal Selection Algorithm: Mimics immune adaptation by cloning high-affinity antibodies (good solutions) and subjecting them to hypermutation. Poor solutions are discarded while mutated clones with improved fitness proliferate. The mutation rate inversely scales with fitness, ensuring focused refinement near promising areas.
Artificial Bee Colony: Divides agents into employed, onlooker, and scout bees performing exploitation, probabilistic following, and random exploration. Employed bees refine current food sources, while scouts replace exhausted sources with new random ones. Control parameters include colony size and abandonment limit, balancing exploration and convergence.
Grey Wolf Optimizer: Uses a hierarchy of alpha, beta, and delta wolves guiding omegas in encircling and attacking prey (optimum). The control coefficient a linearly decreases to transition from exploration to exploitation. Position updates are weighted averages of leader influences, enabling coordinated convergence around the best solutions.
Cuckoo Search: Employs Lévy flights to generate new candidate nests (solutions) and replaces worse hosts with better cuckoo eggs. Discovery probability p_a governs random replacement of poor nests. Heavy-tailed Lévy steps enable long-range exploration while elitist selection intensifies around best solutions.
Bat Algorithm: Uses virtual bats adjusting loudness and pulse rate during flight to balance exploration and exploitation. Loudness decreases and pulse rate increases as bats approach prey (good solutions). Key parameters include frequency range, pulse emission rate, and loudness damping factor controlling convergence speed.
Ant Lion Optimizer: Models ants performing bounded random walks influenced by elite “antlion” traps (promising regions). The random walk range shrinks over time, focusing search around elites while occasional replacements maintain diversity. The algorithm alternates between exploration and intensification guided by adaptive bounds.
Teaching–Learning-Based Optimization: Divides each iteration into a teacher phase (solutions move toward class mean improved by best teacher) and learner phase (pairwise learning between individuals). The teaching factor (1 or 2) controls update magnitude. Alternating global (teacher) and peer learning accelerates convergence toward the optimal solution.
Lion Optimization Algorithm: Organizes solutions into prides and nomads; prides perform local cooperative hunting, while nomads explore globally. Male takeovers and mating events exchange information between groups, maintaining diversity. The balance between pride exploitation and nomadic exploration yields efficient global search.
Harris Hawks Optimization: Mimics cooperative hunting through switching between exploration and exploitation based on prey’s escaping energy. Random perching represents exploration, while coordinated “besiege” and “rapid dive” attacks simulate intensification. The prey energy parameter E decays over iterations, transitioning the swarm naturally to convergence.
Sine Cosine Algorithm: Moves agents in sinusoidal trajectories around the best solution using random sine–cosine updates. The amplitude parameter r_1 decreases linearly to reduce step size, shifting from global oscillations to local fine-tuning. Simple yet powerful, it combines periodic exploration with smooth convergence.
Flower Pollination Algorithm: Alternates between global Lévy flight-based pollination and local pollination within neighborhoods, governed by a switch probability p. Global pollination spreads high-quality traits across the population, while local pollination refines nearby solutions. Lévy step scaling and p determine exploration depth and convergence speed.
Fireworks Algorithm: Simulates explosion-based search where each “firework” spawns sparks with magnitude inversely related to its fitness. Better fireworks create dense local searches; worse ones scatter widely. Control parameters include explosion amplitude and number of sparks, maintaining exploration–exploitation balance.
Differential Search Algorithm: Generates donor vectors via scaled differences between population members and replaces inferior individuals if the new trial is fitter. Its key tuning parameters—scale factor F, crossover rate CR, and population size—govern the diversity and convergence rate. Efficient on continuous, multimodal landscapes.
Coyote Optimization Algorithm: Models social adaptation within small coyote packs sharing a cultural memory (mean position). Coyotes produce offspring through recombination and slight perturbation around pack culture. The weakest are replaced, and occasional migration ensures global exploration. Few parameters yield robust convergence.
Electromagnetic Field Optimization: Treats solutions as charged particles exerting Coulomb forces on each other. Attraction to high-fitness (high-charge) solutions drives exploitation, while repulsion maintains diversity. Velocity damping and charge normalization prevent oscillation and ensure convergence toward equilibrium (optimum).
Vortex Search Algorithm: Samples solutions within shrinking circular regions around the current best point. The vortex radius decreases geometrically each iteration, while random rotation ensures coverage of angular space. This spiral inward contraction efficiently transitions from broad exploration to local refinement.
Thermal Exchange Optimization: Models thermal equilibrium among candidate solutions with different “temperatures” (fitness). Random pairs exchange heat—i.e., blend positions—proportionally to fitness difference until a stable equilibrium (minimum energy) emerges. Cooling and re-heating factors modulate convergence and diversification.
Water Cycle Algorithm: Simulates streams (poor solutions) flowing into rivers and finally the ocean (best solution). Each flow moves proportionally toward its downstream target, improving fitness hierarchically. Occasional “rain” (random reinitialization) maintains diversity and prevents premature convergence.
Artificial Bee Colony: Uses three bee roles (employed, onlooker, scout) to alternate exploitation and exploration. Each employed bee explores near its food source; onlookers probabilistically follow based on quality, and scouts randomly discover new sources. The number of food sources and abandonment limit are key hyperparameters.
Ant Lion Optimizer: Models ant random walks influenced by antlion traps (elite solutions). The random walk radius decreases linearly over iterations, intensifying search near best solutions. Periodic elite replacement ensures adaptation and prevents stagnation.
Grey Wolf Optimizer: Wolves encircle prey via position updates relative to alpha, beta, and delta wolves. The encircling coefficient a decays linearly, switching from exploration to exploitation. The multi-leader hierarchy maintains stability and avoids premature convergence.
